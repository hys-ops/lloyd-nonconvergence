From a midpoint coupling to a nonconvergent Lloyd orbit

A first-moment tool extracted from a long AI proof shortened the Brenier argument and led to an exact Lloyd construction, first in three dimensions and then in every dimension n ≥ 3.

The original proof couples midpoints, then differentiates

Fix distinct sites and perturb the intercepts in opposite directions. For uniform points in positive-volume convex cells P and Q, a coupling bounds the law of their midpoint. Section 4 then sends the intercept perturbation to zero.

ℒ(X+Z2)≤dx|P||Q|.\mathcal L\!\left(\tfrac{X+Z}{2}\right)\leq\frac{dx}{\sqrt{|P|\,|Q|}}.

Compare both endpoints at finite separation

Use two intercept vectors on the same uniform convex body K ⊂ B(0,R). For label i, let aᵢ and bᵢ be its masses and Bᵢ and B′ᵢ its unnormalized first moments. Weight each midpoint by the harmonic mean.

wi=2aibiai+bi,J=∑i(ai−bi)2ai+bi,w_i=\frac{2a_i b_i}{a_i+b_i},\qquad J=\sum_i\frac{(a_i-b_i)^2}{a_i+b_i},
∑i∥Bi−Bi′∥2ai+bi≤R2J.\sum_i\frac{\|B_i-B_i^{\prime}\|^2}{a_i+b_i}\;\leq\;R^2J.

A term with two zero masses is zero. A cell may disappear at one endpoint.

A certificate for an inexact Lloyd step

e=8RJ,ΔEa≤−s2+2es.e=\sqrt{8}\,R\sqrt J,\quad \Delta E_a\le -s^2+2es.

With target masses aᵢ>0, computed centroids zᵢ and current sites yᵢ, let s²=Σᵢaᵢ‖zᵢ−yᵢ‖². For power cells of the same source and sites, s>2e certifies strict descent.

From comparing cell moments to prescribing an exact update

Same quantity, different role

The certificate bounds error in a single approximate move. For exact dynamics, keep the same cell first moment but write it directly as a directional function for an antipodal pair. Its gradient determines the update; the finite harmonic-mean bound is not needed for that identity.

Prescribe the next direction and solve for the gradient

For an even law the cells at ±ru are half-spaces through the origin. Set H(u)=E|u·X|. The next positive site is m(u)=H(u)u+∇ₛH(u). To reach a unit direction v with u·v>0, prescribe this spherical gradient along the orbit.

∇SlogH(u)=vu⋅v−u.\nabla_S\log H(u)=\frac{v}{u\cdot v}-u.

In three dimensions, choose a path that approaches the equatorial circle without settling. Construct a smooth even potential with the prescribed gradients along that path. In higher dimensions, use a path whose limit directions fill the higher-dimensional equator and extend the prescription and realization to the corresponding sphere.

Realize the potential by a probability law

HK(u)=Cn(rn+1)(u)(n+1)|K|.H_K(u)=\frac{C_n(r^{n+1})(u)}{(n+1)|K|}.
Cnq(u)=∫Sn−1|u⋅v|q(v)dS(v).C_nq(u)=\int_{S^{n-1}} |u\cdot v|\,q(v)\,dS(v).

Here r is the radial function. Petty’s directional-moment formula and classical cosine inversion on smooth even functions realize profiles sufficiently close to 1 up to a positive factor. This factor preserves centroid directions.

One orbit accumulates on every equatorial axis

Clust={{±c(w,0)}:w∈Sn−2}≅ℝℙn−2.\operatorname{Clust}=\big\{\{\pm c(w,0)\}:w\in S^{n-2}\big\}\cong\mathbb{RP}^{n-2}.

In every dimension n≥3 there is a smooth positively curved body and an antipodal start with this exact limit set. Heights tend to zero and radii tend to c>0. Each limiting pair is fixed. Forgetting the site labels identifies w and −w. The limit space is a circle for n=3 and the entire real projective plane for n=4.
